An Important Nuance: Sicker Patients Get MORE Monitoring

The raw data shows a counterintuitive pattern: patients who DO have gaps show lower admission rates — often dramatically so.
Care gapAdmission Rate (gap present)Admission Rate (gap absent)
No nutrition counseling5.5%32.6%

No A1C test3.0%9.7%
No creatinine test1.3%9.0%
CONFOUNDING BY INDICATION

Sicker patients receive more monitoring precisely because they are higher-risk. Among nutrition counseling recipients, admitted and non-admitted patients have virtually identical gap burdens (3.8 vs 3.9 out of 11) — what separates them is prior severity: 2× prior admissions, 2× ED visits, +47% charges.

WHAT THE +0.031 GAIN PROVES

If care-gap features were purely redundant with existing severity measures, they would add zero. The largest gain in the project's history demonstrates they carry genuinely new information — but that information operates in multivariate interaction, not as a simple rule.

The relationship is real but complex. The model uses care gaps to identify WHO is at risk — not to prescribe what to do about it. Intervention decisions remain clinical.

A Data Correction Revealed a New Clinical Signal — Insulin Pump Use

In July, CGM use was reported as a meaningful predictor. A data-labeling correction led to a clinically useful discovery.
WHAT HAPPENED

Two insulin pump device codes had been miscounted as CGM sensors. Correcting the device list initially dropped performance—meaning the miscoding had been quietly helping.

WHAT WE DISCOVERED

Insulin pump use is a meaningful, distinct clinical signal. About 10 per 100 pump users were admitted versus about 7 per 100 non-pump users—a one-third higher rate consistent with greater clinical complexity.

RESOLUTION

Corrected the CGM list to sensors only, then built insulin pump use as its own feature. The model recovered and slightly exceeded the July benchmark; Clarity SME validation is in progress.

The July CGM finding stands. Transparent correction produced a more accurate CGM signal and revealed insulin pump use as a second, distinct signal.
