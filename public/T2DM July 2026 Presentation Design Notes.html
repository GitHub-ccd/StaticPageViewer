1. Start with context before visuals

The design work followed the SWD requirement to define who, what, and how before building slides:

SWD question July answer
Who Clinical stakeholders reviewing T2DM unplanned admission model progress
What Show that the current model improved to PR-AUC 0.396, explain what that means operationally, and clarify what remains before deployment
How A live review deck that can also stand alone after the meeting

That context drove the overall tone:
- Clinical language over DS-internal jargon
- Operational meaning over raw metrics alone
- Decision support over technical exhaust

The resulting big idea was effectively:

The current T2DM model improves prioritization meaningfully, but it should be presented as a triage tool and not as a diagnostic system.

Every major slide in the July deck supports that message.

2. Storyboard before building

The July deck follows a SWD-style story arc instead of a data dump.

Narrative arc used

  1. Setup — what the model is and why this matters
  2. Good news first — CGM produced the first measurable gain since June
  3. Operational translation — top-5% lift and threshold choice explain how the model would be used
  4. Credibility and boundaries — two experiments were closed, which increases trust in the remaining direction
  5. Geographic and implementation context — market mix and next steps
  6. Transparency — feature importance shows clinical face validity

This is why the slides were not organized as “all methods first, then all charts, then conclusions.”
Instead, they were sequenced to help a clinician follow the logic from improvement to application to trust.

3. Use the simplest display that fits the message

A core SWD principle is that the chart type should serve the insight, not the other way around.
The July materials used the following display choices intentionally:

Need SWD display choice July implementation
One number matters Large callout number 5.8×, +0.009, 0.396
Ranked comparison Sorted horizontal bar Market distribution slide
Exact threshold choices matter Structured table Operating points and threshold slides
Binary experiment outcome Side-by-side cards Lab trajectory and Milliman closeout slide
Model transparency Feature importance image H10 H2O variable importance slide

Examples of SWD-aligned simplification:
- The CGM result is shown as a green delta callout rather than burying the improvement inside a long metrics table.
- The prioritization story uses a large 5.8× callout because the audience should remember the operational lift more than the underlying formula.
- Threshold selection stays in a table, not a crowded multi-line chart, because exact tradeoffs matter there.

4. Eliminate clutter

The July presentation intentionally removes non-essential visual noise.

SWD clutter reduction choices applied

  • Minimal color palette: blue, green, red, orange, gray
  • No rainbow palette or decorative gradients
  • No 3D charts, donut charts, or overloaded dashboards
  • Limited decimal precision where possible
  • Direct labels and short explanatory text instead of dense legends
  • Large white space around key content so each slide has one focal point

This is visible in the PowerPoint structure:
- Slide titles are short and message-led
- Most slides have only one main visual object
- Tables are compact and formatted for scanning, not for exhaustive detail
- Negative findings are grouped together on one “closed experiments” slide rather than scattered

That is standard SWD behavior: remove anything that competes with the core point.

5. Use preattentive attributes deliberately

SWD teaches that attention should be guided through selective use of color, contrast, size, and position.
The July presentation uses those deliberately.

How attention was directed

SWD attribute July use
Color Green for improvement, red for closed/negative, blue for primary structure, gray for secondary context
Size Large headline numbers such as 5.8× and 0.396
Position Main conclusion placed at top-left or center-left where the eye lands first
Contrast Highlighted callout boxes against soft background fills
Enclosure Key metrics placed in bounded callout cards or takeaway boxes

Important examples:
- Green was reserved for the CGM pass because that is the key positive result.
- Red was reserved for the two closed experiments so the audience can instantly separate “not pursuing” from “working direction.”
- The top 4 markets were accented in the market slide while the rest were muted, so the chart reads as a ranked story, not a flat inventory.

This follows the SWD rule that if everything is highlighted, nothing is highlighted.

6. Turn titles into conclusions, not topics

One of the most important SWD techniques used in the July materials is the shift from topic titles to active-voice conclusion titles.

Examples of the SWD title pattern

Instead of neutral topic labels, the slides were written to state the point:

Topic style SWD message style used in July
Model performance The current model improves precision-recall performance to 0.396
Prioritization Targeting the top 5% is 5.8× more efficient than random outreach
Lab trajectory Snapshot labs outperform trajectory features for this task
Milliman augmentation Adding Milliman-only positives did not improve discrimination
Feature importance Diagnosis burden, prior admissions, and medication complexity are the strongest predictors

That design choice matters because the audience can skim the slide titles alone and still reconstruct the story.

7. Translate technical metrics into clinical meaning

SWD emphasizes adapting the message to the audience. In the July materials, raw ML terminology was translated into language clinicians can act on.

Translation decisions used

  • Precision became PPV in clinical-facing materials
  • Recall became Sensitivity
  • Metrics were often restated in plain English such as:
  • “Of every 100 flagged patients, about X will have an unplanned admission”
  • “Top-5% targeting is 5.8× more efficient than random outreach”
  • The model was framed as a prioritization or triage tool, not as a high-certainty predictor

This is one of the clearest ways the SWD skill shaped the final deliverables: the statistics were not removed, but they were translated into decision language.

8. Good-news-first sequencing was intentional

The July deck leads with the strongest positive result before discussing failure or nuance.
That is an SWD-informed sequencing choice.

Why this was done

If the audience first hears that two experiments failed, they may anchor on disappointment.
If they first hear that the benchmark improved to 0.396 and that top-5% targeting is 5.8× more efficient than random outreach, they have a clearer frame for the later nuance.

So the sequence was:
1. Improvement
2. Operational usefulness
3. Threshold tradeoff
4. What did not work
5. Where the model is strongest
6. What remains next

That is not spin. It is story structure.
It helps the audience retain the central message before absorbing the caveats.

9. Why the feature importance slide was added

The feature importance slide is also consistent with SWD.

Its purpose in the story

The slide is not there to show every predictor.
It is there to answer a credibility question:

Does the model appear to rely on clinically coherent signals?

That is why the feature importance visual belongs near the end of the story.
By that point, the audience already knows the model improved. The remaining question is whether the model is believable.

The slide supports that by showing that the strongest predictors align with recognizable risk themes such as:
- diagnosis burden
- prior admissions
- medication complexity
- monitoring intensity
- social risk

That is a classic SWD move: use the visual to resolve the audience’s next natural question.

10. How the three July assets differ under the same SWD framework

The same SWD ideas were applied differently across the July materials.

Asset SWD emphasis
Clinical Summary notebook Most plain-language, presentation-ready, strongest emphasis on takeaway statements
Technical Update notebook More methodological depth, but still translated for clinicians
PowerPoint notebook + PPTX Most visually compressed, built for fast scanning and live presentation

So the SWD skill was not used to make every asset look identical.
It was used to make each asset appropriate for its audience and format while preserving the same narrative spine.

11. Specific SWD techniques that were visibly used

Techniques applied directly

  • Big-idea-first framing
  • Good-news-first sequencing
  • Active-voice slide titles
  • Large-number callouts for the most important metrics
  • Sorted horizontal bars for ranked comparisons
  • Minimal, purposeful color palette
  • Visual separation of positive vs. closed outcomes
  • Plain-language translation of metrics
  • One main message per slide
  • “So what?” takeaway on major slides

Techniques deliberately avoided

  • Dense multi-chart slides
  • Technical jargon without translation
  • Decorative chart styles
  • Tables with excessive precision
  • Equal emphasis on every result
  • Method details before the audience understands why the result matters

12. Bottom line

The July notebooks and presentation were not just formatted nicely. They were structured around SWD principles:

  1. Start with audience and purpose
  2. Build a story arc before building visuals
  3. Choose the simplest display that serves the point
  4. Remove clutter aggressively
  5. Guide attention with selective color and size
  6. Make titles carry the message
  7. Translate model metrics into operational and clinical meaning

That is how the SWD skill shaped both the content and the presentation mechanics of the July review materials.
