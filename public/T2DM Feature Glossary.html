Detailed Feature Definitions — Top Predictive Dimensions (Rank 1–3)

1. Clinical Diagnoses (87 features)

Diagnoses are collected from 4 independent Epic sources and consolidated through ICD-10 clinical grouping:

Source What It Captures
Medical History Conditions explicitly documented by clinicians during visits
Problem List Provider-curated active conditions under ongoing management
Ambulatory Encounter DX Per-visit coded diagnoses from outpatient encounters
Hospital Account DX Inpatient/ED coded diagnoses from acute-care episodes

All sources are unified into a single patient-level profile. The N_SOURCES_* features below quantify how many sources independently confirm a given condition.

H. Hospital DX Reinforcement (4 features)

Drawn from hospital discharge diagnosis records — conditions documented during acute inpatient episodes, identifying the highest-acuity subgroup.

Features
do_not_resuscitate, other_chronic_obstructive_pulmonary_disease, hypertensive_heart_and_chronic_kidney_disease, heart_failure_hsp

These sharpen severe-hospitalised-subgroup identification and add high-acuity context.

Clinical interpretation: Disease burden and multi-morbidity complexity predict acute decompensation risk. The multi-source architecture ensures diagnostic certainty — a condition confirmed in 3+ sources (Problem List + Medical History + Hospital DX) is far more reliable than a single-source flag. The 4-source consolidation means the model distinguishes incidental coding from established chronic disease.

Features Clinical meaning

Why it matters: Lab features capture active physiologic instability and disease burden. They reflect what is happening in the patient now, not just what has been coded historically.

Clinical interpretation: Lab Results at #2 standalone confirms the model is not driven only by prior utilisation — it detects meaningful physiologic stress directly from laboratory data. A patient with elevated troponin, declining eGFR, and widening anion gap is in active physiologic deterioration regardless of their coding history.

Clinical interpretation: “Past admissions predict future admissions” — patients with established utilisation patterns are at highest risk. The predictor and target use the same definition of unplanned (Emergency/Urgent admission type) across different time windows, which creates a strong and clinically intuitive relationship. This is also the most sample-efficient dimension: only 6 core features achieve near-top performance.

9. Social History / SDOH (27 features)

Important: Unlike other dimensions, social history has no date filter — it captures the patient’s most recent social history ever recorded, regardless of when collected. This is clinically acceptable for stable attributes (education, substance history) but may be stale for dynamic fields (financial strain, food insecurity).

Coverage (Highly Heterogeneous)

Feature Group Coverage Notes
Substance use (alcohol, smoking, tobacco) 94–96% Routinely captured at most encounters
SDOH screening (financial strain, food insecurity) ∼12% Requires specific questionnaire administration
Abuse/safety screening <5% Rarely completed IPV screening
Financial class (payer) ∼99% Near-complete from encounter records

The dimension is effectively a two-tier signal: strong substance-use coverage with moderate lift, plus sparse SDOH screening data with high lift but affecting only ∼12% of patients.

10. BMI (2 features)

Feature Clinical meaning
most_recent_BMI Most recent body mass index, representing current body habitus
BMI_delta Change in BMI over time, representing weight gain or weight loss trajectory

Counterintuitive Findings (Important for Clinical Interpretation)

BMI Category % of Cohort Positive Rate Lift
Missing BMI 27% 1.3% 0.18× (strongly protective)
Underweight (<18.5) 0.4% 16.6% 2.21× (highest risk)
Normal (18.5–24.9) 8.5% 12.4% 1.65×
Overweight (25–29.9) 19% 9.8% 1.30×
Obese I–III (30+) 45% 9.0–9.4% 1.20–1.34×

Why this is counterintuitive in a T2DM population: (1) Missing BMI is protective because it signals low healthcare engagement (fewer encounters = lower acuity). (2) Underweight/normal BMI in T2DM often indicates cachexia, advanced disease, or unintentional weight loss — NOT healthy body composition. (3) The obese majority clusters in a narrow lift range with minimal differentiation.

Clinical interpretation: BMI trajectory (BMI_delta) captures meaningful physiologic change — both rapid weight loss (cachexia, advanced disease) and rapid weight gain (fluid overload, decompensation) are clinically concerning. The pair of features is simple but additive.
