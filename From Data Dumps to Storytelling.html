Part 2 · What SWD is
SWD is six moves done in order — most bad decks skip the first two
Move 1
Understand the context
Who, what, how — then one Big Idea sentence.
Move 2
Storyboard before building
Setup → tension → resolution, in bullets first.
Move 3
Choose the right display
The simplest display that shows the finding.
Move 4
Eliminate clutter
If removing it changes nothing, remove it.
Move 5
Direct attention
One accent; everything else gray.
Move 6
Tell a story on every slide
The title states the finding; a takeaway closes it.

Remove
  • Borders and heavy gridlines
  • Tick marks and repeated axis titles
  • Legends — label the series directly
  • Extra decimals: 7.5%, not 7.47%
  • Rainbow palettes and ALL CAPS
Never: 3D, dual axes, pies over five slices, donuts, radar charts

Part 3 · Trust is the product
In clinical ML, trust is the product — and honest framing is how you build it
Transparency about failures — closed experiments shown, not buried
Clinical face validity — top features make medical sense
Operational translation — their workflow, not our metrics
Appropriate humility — “triage tool, not diagnostic”
Good-news-first sequencing — story structure, not spin
Say the limit out loud
Fewer than half of top-5% patients are admitted. PPV passes 50% only in about the top 1%.
It’s strong prioritization, not high accuracy.

Part 3 · Two rules that cannot be broken
Two vocabulary rules are now hard-coded into every clinical document
1
Name the production model, never the benchmark tool
Clinical documents say H2O DAI. XGBoost is our internal benchmarking framework — mentioning it makes people ask which model is real.
2
Say PPV and Sensitivity, never Precision and Recall
Clinicians trained in epidemiology already know PPV and Sensitivity. Precision and Recall are ML jargon for the same ideas.

Part 5 · The hardest SWD test
A finding that contradicts intuition needs the full setup → tension → resolution arc
Setup
You’d expect patients missing expected diabetes care to be admitted more often.
Tension
5.5% vs. 32.6%
Admission rates without vs. with nutrition counseling — the opposite of intuition.
Resolution
Confounding by indication: sicker patients get more care. The features still gave the project’s largest gain (+0.031) — the model uses them to find who needs attention, not what to do.

Appendix · From the SWD skill
Ten checks run before any deck is published
  1. Big Idea written and shown on the first slide
  2. Every title is active voice and states the finding
  3. Clutter removed from every chart
  4. One preattentive attribute per chart
  5. Every technical metric translated
  6. Setup → tension → resolution intact end to end
  7. Explicit takeaway on each key slide
  8. Market data uses the Table 5.2 fallback logic
  9. Experiment deltas shown vs. the current benchmark
  10. Code cells hidden for the audience view
